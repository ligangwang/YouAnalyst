# YouAnalyst website improvement review — September 9, 2026

The highest-value direction is a coherent company-research workflow: find a company, understand its relationships, inspect evidence, save the research, and return when there is something useful to review. The existing map and company pages provide a strong foundation. Data interpretation and avoidable friction deserve attention before adding more breadth.

This document records the original audit and proposed backlog. Implementation began afterward; see [implementation progress](website-improvements-progress.md) for completed local changes and remaining work, and [insider value review holds](insider-value-quality.md) for the first data-quality fix. Production has not been changed by this work.

**Scope and confidence**

Reviewed the live homepage, NVIDIA and IHT company pages, prediction feed, community watchlists, institution discovery, and all three daily sections. Tested company selection, an evidence panel, map/list switching, search feedback, and the signed-in company-save link. Inspected desktop and a narrow mobile viewport (390 × 844 override; 375 CSS pixels of content width after scrollbar). Reviewed the corresponding source, authentication UI, methodology page, analytics, sitemap, and data-processing code.

The browser had an existing signed-in session. Account creation, signed-out conversion, private-account behavior, administrative workflows, and every company/filing were not tested end to end. No account changes, predictions, follows, or messages were submitted. No Lighthouse, field performance, formal accessibility, penetration, or load audit was performed. Recommendations below distinguish observed behavior, source-code findings, and proposed additions. Local source is supporting evidence, not proof of the exact deployed revision. Web retrieval could not read the site reliably; direct browser inspection worked, including the SEC filing.

**Product, navigation, and visual improvements**

1. **Provide a guided research example.** The starter buttons currently use the first three covered tickers: AAPL, AMD, AMZN in this visit. Offer an evidence-backed starting question such as “Who supplies NVIDIA?” that opens the relevant relationship. Keep it factual and sourced rather than turning it into an investment recommendation.
2. **Unify company search.** Searching “Berkshire” on the homepage yields “No match on this page” and redirects the visitor conceptually to another search surface. Reuse a global company lookup, distinguish “known company, no relationship coverage” from “not found,” and support keyboard selection and ticker aliases.
3. **Simplify the map toolbar.** Hide Previous/Page 1/Next when neither direction is available. Group secondary filters under an expandable control. Keep search, current company, view mode, and a clear reset visible.
4. **Improve graph orientation.** Default to a readable selected-company neighborhood for deep links; group suppliers, customers, and competitors; make selected edges more prominent. Offer full names on focus as well as hover. Retain the existing list alternative and keyboard-operable SVG nodes.
5. **Make evidence easier to inspect.** TSMC → NVIDIA correctly opens a dated excerpt, but its source link goes to a filing index. Link to the document and section when available, retain the accession link, and show issuer, filing date, extraction date, identity-review state, and source type together.
6. **Explain coverage in user language.** The overview shows 10/28 companies with published extraction, 62 connections outside the preview, and extraction updated May 9. These are different concepts. Distinguish “has its own filing,” “mentioned by another filing,” and “reviewed industry research.” A pending company can still have evidence-backed incoming relationships. Do not interpret an old extraction date as proof that all relationships are obsolete.
7. **Increase text and control readability.** Map CSS uses 10–12px for many controls, legends, and details. Increase frequently read text and tap areas; reserve tiny text for secondary metadata. Test contrast and keyboard focus with actual measurements before claiming accessibility compliance.
8. **Replace overflowing mobile navigation.** The screenshot exposes a horizontal scrollbar and hides later destinations. Provide a compact primary navigation plus an accessible More menu. Add visible current-page state; the inspected navigation has no `aria-current` treatment.
9. **Unify saved research.** Map-saved companies, public watchlists, and followed institutions are separate concepts. Give them a clear common entry point, explain which are private/public, and allow a saved company to open either its map or company research. Do not silently change existing visibility.
10. **Add trust and help navigation.** The inspected footer has a disclaimer and feedback link. Make About, data methodology, coverage, privacy, terms, and support discoverable. Explain what an account stores and what other users can see. Draft policy content from actual product behavior rather than generic promises.

**Page-specific opportunities**

11. **Restore the reason behind a prediction.** The live feed cards show direction, return, author, and level but no thesis. `thesisTitle` and `thesis` exist in the feed model but are not rendered. Show a title or short excerpt, entry date, and price-as-of date, with a clear path to the full argument.
12. **Avoid implying recent community publishing.** The first live feed item was 132 days old. Existing positions can still receive fresh end-of-day marks: the NVDA page showed a September 8 mark. Distinguish publication recency from valuation freshness; add filters for recent research, active calls, settled calls, and followed authors. Label AI accounts explicitly where identity otherwise depends on the handle.
13. **Put institution discovery before empty personalization.** For an account without follows, three empty sections precede recent ticker activity. Replace them with one concise onboarding panel and suggested institutions from covered data; reveal digest controls when they become useful. Remove “Dry runs” from normal research UX or restrict it to an operational view.
14. **Clarify institutional metrics.** Preserve report date and filing date, which are already shown. Explain the ranking window, coverage, and difference between share change and reported dollar-value change. Avoid “Net buying/selling” labels if the underlying metric is a value change that also reflects valuation or missing comparison data.
15. **Make company pages easier to scan.** NVDA lists many individual insider transaction rows, including several from the same insider and filing. Add summary totals and expandable filing/insider groups, with raw rows retained for verification. Keep relationship, insider, and institution anchors available while scrolling and preserve price precision in detail views.
16. **Clarify daily rankings.** The Top Calls page repeats AMD across several authors/calls. Offer a grouped-by-company view while preserving individual results. Explain ranking basis beside the result, including why percentage change and score change can produce different impressions. Daily institutional and insider pages also repeat their page title/description within the first section; remove the duplication.
17. **Strengthen watchlist discovery.** Community watchlists show returns and calls but little explanation of purpose. Add owner-written descriptions, coverage, activity dates, and honest comparison context. Keep the user's own research easy to find instead of making community performance the only entry.
18. **Complete account recovery and form feedback.** The inspected auth component has Google/email paths and autocomplete, but no password-recovery control, visible persistent field labels, or password visibility toggle. Add those, readable error messages, and accessible error announcements. Verify signup and save continuation in a disposable test account/staging environment.

**Technical and measurement improvements**

19. **Improve initial loading without conflating it with missing data.** The homepage initially shows zero nodes and zero coverage before its client fetch resolves. Render a meaningful server-provided preview or skeleton, then enhance it into the interactive graph. Public company overview content is already server-rendered; preserve that strength. Graph API caching and a client timeout/retry already exist.
20. **Fix feed error states.** In source, initial load failure also satisfies the “No predictions yet” condition. A non-OK Load more response silently returns, and a network rejection has no user-facing catch. Separate empty, failed, and loaded states; retain prior items and offer a retry. These failure cases were identified in code, not induced against production.
21. **Audit keyboard and screen-reader behavior.** Three digest selects lack accessible labels. Navigation menus lack Escape/focus-management handling in the inspected component, and no sitewide skip link was found. Label inputs, test focus return and tab order, and provide a short keyboard path through dense graph content. Existing map node labels and list view should be retained.
22. **Align search and sharing metadata.** Company routes already have canonical URLs, server-rendered evidence, and structured data. Extend that work with a coherent site description, a dedicated homepage social image, and useful evidence-backed company/industry landing content. Do not mass-generate thin pages for uncovered symbols.
23. **Make sitemap generation resilient as coverage grows.** The source has multiple 500-record caps and one broad catch that can drop dynamic route groups after an unrelated query failure. Add independent failure handling, monitoring, and paginated sitemap indexes when needed. `/predictions` and the three named daily section roots are absent from the static route list; include intended indexable entry pages. Live sitemap contents were not independently verified.
24. **Measure the real funnel.** Graph interaction and auth events already exist. Track successful source reading, save completion, and return visits alongside signup. Sitewide external registrations remain the conversion outcome; saved research and published calls are distinct activation measures. Use the existing conversion-sprint plan, exclude internal testers, and reconcile event counts with account records. This audit did not inspect GA4 or establish conversion rates.
25. **Measure performance before optimizing broadly.** Capture production field data where available and run mobile lab checks for LCP, INP, and CLS. Inspect map payload size, cold starts, auth-dependent public loading, and duplicate requests. No performance score or timing improvement is claimed here.
26. **Add data-quality observability.** Track coverage by source, latest successful ingest, unresolved identities, anomalous prices, baseline completeness, and failed derived summaries. Retain raw source references and correction history. Add regression fixtures for actual anomalies before changing parsing or comparison rules.

**Suggested delivery order**

1. Validate and contain questionable ranking data; distinguish unavailable 13F baselines from new positions. Verify the IHT case without inventing corrected values.
2. Fix company saving, feed error states, missing labels, and irrelevant pagination. Test signed-in and signed-out save journeys.
3. Simplify mobile entry, improve text sizes, unify search, and put institution discovery ahead of empty panels.
4. Align positioning and methodology, restore thesis context in the feed, and improve evidence links and freshness explanations.
5. Review actual external-user funnel counts and interview a few new visitors. Choose one retention addition based on observed behavior.

Success should be measured by visitors finding a relevant company, understanding a sourced relationship, completing a save, and returning voluntarily. Additions should earn their place by helping those actions, not by increasing the feature count.
